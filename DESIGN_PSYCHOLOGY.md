# Design Psychology

## The Science Behind "Staying"

This document explains the psychological principles that make this minimalist experience compelling.

---

## Core Psychological Mechanisms

### 1. **Curiosity Gap**
> "Maybe something happens if I stay longer..."

**Theory:** Information gap theory (Loewenstein, 1994)
- Players arrive expecting gameplay
- The absence creates a knowledge gap
- The gap generates curiosity that keeps them engaged

**Application:**
- Messages are spaced far apart (30s, 2min, 5min...)
- Each message hints at "more" without promising anything
- Players stay to close the gap

### 2. **Sunk Cost Fallacy**
> "I've already stayed 10 minutes. Might as well keep going."

**Theory:** Behavioral economics (Arkes & Blumer, 1985)
- People continue investing in something based on past investment
- Time already spent becomes a reason to keep spending time
- Rational choice would be to leave, but psychology prevents it

**Application:**
- Timer isn't shown, but players know time has passed
- Messages remind them: "Most people would have left by now"
- Creates pressure to justify the time already invested

### 3. **Absence as Presence**
> "The lack of content IS the content."

**Theory:** Negative space in art/design
- What's missing becomes more important than what's there
- Players project meaning onto emptiness
- The room becomes a canvas for reflection

**Application:**
- No music makes silence meaningful
- No objectives makes staying a choice
- No rewards makes time the only currency

### 4. **Social Proof (Inverted)**
> "If others are leaving... should I stay to be different?"

**Theory:** Conformity and contrarianism (Asch, 1951)
- Fake disconnect messages suggest others leave
- Some players want to be different
- "I'll be the one who stays" becomes motivation

**Application:**
- Leaderboard shows "People Who Stayed"
- Being listed becomes a badge of defiance
- Social comparison drives behavior

### 5. **Variable Ratio Reinforcement**
> "Maybe the NEXT message will be different..."

**Theory:** Operant conditioning (Skinner, 1953)
- Unpredictable rewards create strongest engagement
- Slot machines use this principle
- Players keep "pulling the lever" (staying) for the next message

**Application:**
- Messages arrive at increasing intervals
- Each message is slightly different in tone
- Players anticipate variation

### 6. **Reactance Theory**
> "You're telling me to leave? Now I HAVE to stay."

**Theory:** Psychological reactance (Brehm, 1966)
- When freedom is threatened, people resist
- Being told what to do triggers opposition
- The game never explicitly says "stay," which paradoxically works

**Application:**
- "You can leave whenever you want" (triggers defiance)
- "Staying won't make this better" (challenge accepted)
- Leave button appears but players resist using it

### 7. **Existential Reflection**
> "Why AM I still here?"

**Theory:** Terror management theory (Greenberg et al., 1986)
- Confronting meaninglessness triggers self-reflection
- Players question their choices
- The game becomes a mirror

**Application:**
- No distractions force introspection
- Messages address the player directly
- The room becomes a meditation on choice

## Message Design Strategy

### Tone Progression

| Phase | Tone | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| **0-2min** | Observational | Establish the game's awareness |
| **2-10min** | Analytical | Make players question their behavior |
| **10-20min** | Provocative | Challenge the decision to stay |
| **20min+** | Resigned | Accept the player's choice |

### Key Phrases Analysis

**"You're still here."**
- Present tense = ongoing observation
- No judgment, just acknowledgment
- Persistent presence creates discomfort

**"Most people would have left by now."**
- Creates social comparison
- Implies abnormality (curiosity or defiance)
- First explicit acknowledgment of time passed

**"Nothing is happening because you're waiting."**
- Shifts responsibility to player
- Reframes waiting as a choice
- The player IS the obstacle

**"Staying won't make this better."**
- Removes hope
- Honest but provocative
- Tests player's commitment

**"You can leave whenever you want."**
- Reasserts agency
- Paradoxically makes leaving harder
- Freedom highlighted = freedom questioned

**"Why didn't you?"**
- Confrontational
- Forces self-examination
- No right answer

**"We know how long you stayed."**
- Surveillance implication
- Permanent record
- Actions have been noted

### Return Visit Messages

**"You came back."**
- Surprise mixed with judgment
- Acknowledges abnormal behavior
- No one "should" come back

**"Last time you stayed X minutes."**
- Quantifies the "waste"
- Makes time investment explicit
- Shame or pride, depending on player

**"You didn't have to."**
- Emphasizes choice
- Implies pointlessness
- Deepest psychological cut

## Monetization Psychology

### "Silence" Gamepass

**Psychological Function:**
- For players who WANT the void
- Removes reminders = removes guilt
- Paradox: Paying for less content

**Target Audience:**
- Players seeking meditation
- Those who found messages annoying
- Meta-players who appreciate the concept

### "Support Logging" (Donations)

**Psychological Function:**
- Tests if players seek reward
- Exposes expectation of transaction
- "What do I get?" reveals mindset

**Effect:**
- Global message removes individual recognition
- Cold, distant acknowledgment
- Players feel the futility

### "Acknowledged"

**Psychological Function:**
- Players paying for recognition
- One-time limit makes it scarce
- Cold response provides no warmth

**Effect:**
- "Input acknowledged" is bureaucratic
- No personalization
- Transactional emptiness

### "View Partial Log"

**Psychological Function:**
- Desire for self-knowledge
- Quantified self movement
- Control through data

**Effect:**
- "Some entries unavailable" creates frustration
- Incomplete transparency
- You can't fully know yourself

### "System Load"

**Psychological Function:**
- Collective guilt experiment
- Players affect others (illusion)
- Moral dimension of choice

**Effect:**
- Global consequence
- No visible change
- Question: Did I do something wrong?

### "Why"

**Psychological Function:**
- Meta-commentary on purchasing
- Players recognize the absurdity
- Buying it = being in on the joke

**Effect:**
- Literally nothing
- The perfect product for this game
- Philosophy as purchase

## Ethical Considerations

### What Makes This Ethical

1. **Transparency**
   - Products clearly state their (non)effects
   - No deception about functionality
   - Players know what they're buying

2. **Respect for Agency**
   - Game never forces staying
   - Leave button is always available (after 15min)
   - No manipulation to prevent leaving

3. **No Exploitation**
   - No addiction mechanics (dailies, streaks)
   - No FOMO (fear of missing out)
   - No randomized rewards (loot boxes)

4. **Artistic Intent**
   - Designed as art/commentary
   - Not cynical profit-seeking
   - Educational about game psychology

### What Could Be Problematic

1. **Time Waste**
   - Players may genuinely waste time
   - Mitigation: Game is honest about this

2. **Confusion**
   - Some players may feel deceived
   - Mitigation: Clear game description

3. **Purchasing Psychology**
   - Some may buy products to "see what happens"
   - Mitigation: Clear product descriptions

## Player Archetypes

### The Curious
- Stays to see what happens
- Leaves around 5-10 minutes
- Comments: "Interesting concept"

### The Defiant
- Stays because messages tell them to leave
- Stays 15-30 minutes
- Comments: "I'll stay as long as I want!"

### The Philosopher
- Stays for introspection
- May stay 30+ minutes
- Comments: "This made me think..."

### The Completionist
- Wants to "beat" the game
- Stays until final message
- Comments: "Is there an ending?"

### The Confused
- Doesn't understand the concept
- Leaves in 0-2 minutes
- Comments: "Broken game?"

### The Meta-Player
- Understands it's art/commentary
- Stays to appreciate the design
- Comments: "This is brilliant"

## Expected Metrics

### Session Length Distribution
- **30% quit in 0-1 minute** (confusion)
- **40% quit in 1-5 minutes** (curiosity satisfied)
- **20% quit in 5-15 minutes** (commitment tested)
- **8% stay 15-30 minutes** (defiance/investment)
- **2% stay 30+ minutes** (philosophy/meditation)

### Retention
- **Day 1:** 15-25% (return to confirm it's "real")
- **Day 7:** 5-10% (genuinely intrigued)
- **Day 30:** 2-5% (committed to the concept)

### Monetization
- **Silence:** 5-10% of 30+ minute players
- **Donations:** 2-5% of all players (curiosity)
- **Why:** 10-15% of engaged players (it's cheap and meta)

## Comparisons to Other Games

### The Stanley Parable
- **Similarity:** Choice as mechanic, narrator commentary
- **Difference:** This game has no narrator, no branching paths

### Getting Over It with Bennett Foddy
- **Similarity:** Frustration as design, philosophical commentary
- **Difference:** No skill requirement, just time

### Universal Paperclips
- **Similarity:** Incremental commitment, questioning goals
- **Difference:** No progression, no expansion

### Cookie Clicker
- **Similarity:** Time investment, minimal interaction
- **Difference:** No growth, no dopamine hits

### Journey
- **Similarity:** Minimalist, emotional, mysterious
- **Difference:** No beauty, no movement, just stillness

## Design Philosophy Summary

> **The game is honest.**
> It doesn't pretend to be fun.
> It doesn't promise rewards.
> It doesn't manipulate you to stay.
>
> **It simply observes.**
>
> And by observing, it makes you observe yourself.
> By offering nothing, it reveals what you seek.
> By being honest, it becomes profound.
